<objective>
The two lessons whose requirements are written as negatives: lesson 2 only completes after circle,
square and triangle in that order, and lesson 3 only completes after its targets are activated in
its defined order.

The archived lesson 2 is the reason those requirements are worded that way. It declared an array
naming the expected order and never read it, so a child could touch the shapes in any order and the
game congratulated them. The fix is not a better comparison. In these two lessons the order lives in
an array of the target nodes themselves and that array is *used*: each completion activates the next
element, and every other target is inactive and structurally unable to complete. There is no
identifier comparison in either orchestrator, deliberately — a comparison is the thing that was
declared and forgotten, and leaving one in would invite the next reader to believe it is the
mechanism.

Each lesson therefore gets an explicit out-of-order case that proves the negative directly: a touch
on a shape whose turn has not come emits that target's refusal signal, completes nothing, and moves
the progress label not at all. A case that only drove the correct order would pass on the archived
code too.

Purpose: closes LESSON-02 and LESSON-03, grows the lesson table to three, and adds two more real
completions to the progress file. Plan 03-05 then adds lessons 4 and 5 on the same two patterns.

Output: `scenes/lesson_2.tscn` and `scripts/lesson_2.gd`, `scenes/lesson_3.tscn` and
`scripts/lesson_3.gd`, two new entries in the lesson table, and two new cases in
`scripts/tools/probe_lesson_order.gd`.

Decisions implemented: D-33, D-34, D-35, D-37, D-38, D-45, D-46.
</objective>
