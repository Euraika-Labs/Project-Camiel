<objective>
Close Phase 2 the way the user decided: run every automated gate on the finished tree, put the
real game in front of a person, and apply their verdict.

D-30 exists for the same reason Phase 1's playtest did. Two things in this phase's scope are
structurally unreachable from a headless run. A tap and a click on a real pointer device
cannot be synthesised meaningfully — the probes prove the accept action on a focused control
reaches the same single signal, which is the structural half of the claim, but only a person
with a mouse or a touchscreen closes the other half. And audibility is not playback state: the
headless run uses a null audio driver, which tracks whether a stream is playing perfectly well
and produces no sound at all, so "the sound volume control has an audible effect" (INTRO-06)
has no headless proof and never will.

Purpose: the phase's requirements are all asserted automatically by the time this plan starts.
This plan is what converts those assertions into a claim about the experience a three-year-old
actually has.

Output: a recorded verdict, any tuning it calls for applied to already-exported values, and a
green final gate.

Decisions implemented: D-30.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:human-verify" gate="blocking-human">
  <name>Task 2: The user plays the whole loop once and records the verdict (D-30)</name>
  <what-built>
The game window opens on the title screen: a soft blue background, the word `Camiel` in large text, and one big cream button reading `Spelen` with an orange play triangle on it. Background music should be playing from the moment the window opens.

Pressing it goes to the main menu: the same blue background, a `Start` button with a walking-footprints icon, and below it a cream panel with two rows — a speaker icon next to `Geluid` with a wide slider, and a music-note icon next to `Muziek` with a second wide slider.

Pressing `Start` drops you into the 3D room. It is a square space with walls on all four sides, a low block off to one side to jump onto, an orange glowing sphere floating somewhere between you and a green glowing ring. Camiel is the orange capsule with a dark nose. Arrow keys or W, A, S and D move him, Space jumps.

Walking into the sphere makes it pulse, fade away and play a chime. Walking through the green ring fades up a cream panel over the room reading `Goed zo!` with two buttons: `Nog een keer` with a loop arrow, and `Naar menu` with a house.
  </what-built>
  <how-to-verify>
    1. On the title screen, click `Spelen` with the mouse. Expected: you land on the main menu once, immediately, with no flicker and no double jump.
    2. Come back to the title screen (restart the game if needed) and this time press Enter without touching the mouse. Expected: exactly the same result. Both routes must feel identical.
    3. On the main menu, drag the `Geluid` slider from high to low and back. Expected: the sound effects get quieter and louder. Then press `Start`, collect the sphere, and confirm the chime volume matches where you left the slider.
    4. Back on the menu, drag the `Muziek` slider. Expected: the background music alone changes, and the effect chime stays where the `Geluid` slider put it. If moving one slider moves both, stop and say so — that is the defect this phase exists to fix.
    5. Listen to the music for at least thirty seconds. Expected: it repeats without a gap, a click or a stop.
    6. In the room, walk in every direction and jump onto the low block. Expected: a slow, calm pace, the camera staying gently behind Camiel, nothing flashing or jolting.
    7. Walk hard into each of the four walls. Expected: Camiel stops, stays on the floor, and never falls out of the room or gets stuck in a corner.
    8. Collect the sphere. Expected: one pulse, one fade, one chime. If you hear the chime twice, or hear nothing at all, stop and say so.
    9. Walk through the green ring. Expected: the win panel fades up — never a hard cut — and Camiel stops moving.
    10. Press `Nog een keer` by mouse. Expected: the panel disappears, you are back at the start of the same room, and the sphere is back where it was. Collect it and reach the ring a second time to confirm the win panel appears again.
    11. Press `Naar menu`, this time by pressing Tab until it has an orange ring around it and then Enter. Expected: you land on the menu once, and both sliders show where you left them rather than snapping back.
    12. Judge the whole thing for a child around age 3. Are the buttons big enough to hit without aiming? Is anything startling, loud, or fast? Are the sliders reachable without being so easy to hit by accident that they get in the way?
  </how-to-verify>
  <resume-signal>Reply "looks good" if nothing needs changing, or name each thing to adjust in plain words — for example "the sphere spins too fast", "music is too loud by default", "the buttons are too small", "the room is too big", "the win panel fades in too slowly", or "walking is too quick"</resume-signal>
</task>
